Axioms are basic facts accepted as true without proof. The axioms used in this lesson are:

  1. Quantities which are equal to the same quantity are equal to one another.
  2. If equals are added to equals, the sums are equal.
  3. If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal.
  4. If equals are multiplied by equals, the products are equal.
  5. If equals are divided by equals (not zero), the quotients are equal.
  6. The whole is greater than any of its parts.

Question 3

(i) x + 7 − 7 = 12 − 7 (equals subtracted from equals) → x = 5

(ii) x/3 × 3 = 4 × 3 (equals multiplied by equals) → x = 12

(iii) 5x − 4 + 4 = 21 + 4 (equals added to equals) → 5x = 25;   5x ÷ 5 = 25 ÷ 5 (equals divided by equals) → x = 5

(iv) 2(x + 3) ÷ 2 = 18 ÷ 2 (equals divided by equals) → x + 3 = 9;   x + 3 − 3 = 9 − 3 (equals subtracted from equals) → x = 6

Question 4

AB = CD (given). Add BC to both sides: AB + BC = CD + BC (equals added to equals).

But AB + BC = AC and BC + CD = BD. Therefore AC = BD.

Question 5

PR = QS (given), that is PQ + QR = QR + RS.

Subtract QR from both sides: PQ + QR − QR = QR + RS − QR (equals subtracted from equals). Therefore PQ = RS.

Question 6

AÔB = CÔD (given). Add BÔC to both sides: AÔB + BÔC = CÔD + BÔC (equals added to equals).

But AÔB + BÔC = AÔC and BÔC + CÔD = BÔD. Therefore AÔC = BÔD.

Question 7

Since PQ̂R = PR̂Q, dividing both by 2: ½ PQ̂R = ½ PR̂Q (equals divided by equals). Therefore SQ̂R = TR̂Q.

Question 9

(i) AB = AC and AC = BC, so AB and BC are both equal to AC. Therefore AB = BC (equal to the same quantity).

(ii) AB = BC = AC, so ABC is an equilateral triangle.

(iii) AC = AB + BC, so AB is a part of AC. Therefore AC > AB (the whole is greater than the part).
