Source: scanned past paper (32-S-I = Paper I, 32-S-II = Paper II). This is a blueprint of slots/natures, not a copy. ## 1. Paper I (Mathematics I) - cover and rules

- Title block: Dept of Examinations, Sri Lanka; G.C.E. (Ord. Level) Examination 2024(2025); Mathematics I; code 32 S I; Time: Two hours. 8 pages (cover + pp 2-8).
- Top box: "Index number" line, "Certified correct" + invigilator signature line.
- "Important" instructions (translated):
  - This question paper has 8 pages.
  - Write your index number correctly in the places provided on this page and on the third page.
  - Answer ALL questions on this question paper itself.
  - Use only the space provided under each question for writing the answer and for showing how you obtained it.
  - Show the relevant steps and correct units when answering.
  - Marks are given as follows: Part A - 2 marks per question; Part B - 10 marks per question.
  - Scrap paper may be obtained for calculations.
- Examiners' use table: Part A (questions 1-25) | Part B rows 1,2,3,4,5 | total; columns "Question no. / Marks"; below it boxes: First examiner (name + code no.), Second examiner, Maths examiner, Chief examiner (each with "marker code").
- Running header per page: "OL/2024(2025)/32/S-I", page number, and index number line from p3 on. Footer "[Turn over page]". Part A pages carry a small box bottom-right (page marks); last Part A page shows circled "50"; each Part B question has circled "10".
- Part A heading: "Answer all questions on this paper itself." Note under it: take pi = 22/7.
- Part B heading: "Answer all questions on this paper itself."
- Answers written on the paper in the blank space under each question (Part A: boxed cells ~ 5-6 cm high; diagrams sit right of the text). Ends "* * *".
- Total: 25 x 2 = 50 + 5 x 10 = 50 = 100.

## 3. Paper I Part B (Q1-5, 10 marks each; ALL compulsory; answer on paper)

Marks per sub-part are not printed; "(est.)" splits sum to 10.

### B1 - Income tax / fractions / percentage (Grade 10-11: Taxes, Fractions, Percentages)
Context: Janaka pays income tax of 6% on the part of his monthly salary exceeding Rs 100 000. After paying tax, 1/6 of the money he receives goes on food; 3/5 of the remainder goes on other expenses.
- (i) What fraction of the received money remains after food - 1 (est.)
- (ii) What fraction of the received money remains after food + other expenses - 2
- (iii) Given the final remaining amount (Rs 39 600), find the money spent on food and on other expenses separately - 2
- (iv) Find his salary before tax - 3
- (v) If the tax threshold is raised so that he is exempt from tax and the food spend (amount) stays the same, food spend is what % of the salary - 2
No diagram.

### B2 - (a) Distance-time graph (Grade 10: graphs of motion); (b) Perimeter & area of composite plane figure (Grade 10)
- (a) Context: a person travels from home to a friend's house at a uniform speed for 30 min, stays 30 min, returns by the same route at another speed for 45 min. A grid (axes: time in hours 0-2, distance in km 0-35, dashed grid, first segment already drawn) is provided.
  - (i) Complete the distance-time graph using the given information - 2-3
  - (ii) Find the return speed in km/h - 1-2
- (b) Figure: semicircle AEB (diameter given, 28 cm) joined to rectangle ABCD (one side 77 cm, right angle marked at C). pi = 22/7 (given on A-page header).
  - (i) Perimeter of the composite figure - 2-3
  - (ii) Area of rectangle is how many times the area of the semicircle - 2-3
Diagram: grid with partial graph + composite figure.

### B3 - Rates (local tax), maintenance, shares & dividends (Grade 11)
Context: Kamal rents out a house at Rs 8000/month and collects the year's rent in one payment. The municipal council assesses the house's annual value at Rs 12 000 and charges annual rates of 18% of it. 10% of the rent is spent on maintenance. He then adds more money to the remainder and buys shares of Rs 50 each; each share pays Rs 2.50 dividend per year; he receives Rs 6000 dividends after one year.
- (i) Annual rates payable - 2
- (ii) Money left after paying rates and maintenance - 2
- (iii)(a) Number of shares bought (6000 / 2.50 gives dividend per share count) - 3
- (iii)(b) The extra money he added as a percentage of the total invested - 3
No diagram.

### B4 - Statistics: frequency table, histogram/polygon, cumulative frequency, probability (Grade 10-11)
Context: marks of a group of students shown as a frequency polygon (grid with x axis marks 0-100, y axis number of students 0-90, plotted points at class midpoints) and an incomplete table (columns: marks class, number of students, cumulative frequency; classes 10-20, 20-30, 30-50, 50-60, 60-90 - UNEQUAL class widths; first row filled, a few cells filled, rest dotted).
- (i) Complete the frequency column using the polygon, i.e. unequal class widths (histogram heights) - 3
- (ii) Complete the histogram/ the corresponding frequency graph on the given grid (note: part wording "draw the grid line/histogram bars on the given graph paper") - 3
- (iii) Complete the cumulative frequency column - 2
- (iv) Probability that a randomly chosen student scored 50 or more - 2
Note: table is partially pre-filled; polygon pre-drawn. Same-width trap: classes of width 10 and 20/30.

### B5 - Probability: sample-space grid, tree diagram (Grade 11)
Context: for a school sport a squad of grade 10 (four boys B1-B4 + one girl G1) and a squad of grade 11 (two boys B5, B6 + three girls G2-G4); one from each grade is chosen at random for the first match round. A 5x5 dashed grid is given (columns grade 10: B1 B2 B3 B4 G1; rows grade 11: B5 B6 G2 G3 G4).
- (i) Considering the two-person selection, mark the sample space (all outcomes) on the grid with 'X' - 2
- (ii) Enclose with a closed curve the outcomes where exactly one boy is selected; give that probability - 3
- (iii) Given: if exactly one boy chosen, grade 10 win probability 4/10; if both boys or both girls, 3/10; complete probabilities on the incomplete tree diagram (branches "exactly one boy" / "both boys or both girls" -> "grade 10 wins" / "does not win") - 3
- (iv) Probability grade 10 does not win - 2
Diagram: grid and tree both provided.

Part B overall: B1 fractions/percent/tax, B2 graph + composite mensuration, B3 shares/tax finance, B4 statistics tables/graphs, B5 probability. (Order is not fixed in the unit spacing but this year: finance, motion graph + mensuration, finance2, statistics, probability.)

## 5. Paper II Part A (choose 5 of Q1-6; each 10 marks; sub-part marks not printed)

### A1 - Hire purchase / loans / interest comparison (Grade 11: Interest, annual rates, instalments)
Context: price Rs 84 000 television (price given); two buying methods in a two-column boxed text: Person 1 takes a loan of the full price on annual compound/simple interest for 1 year and pays a lump sum loan settlement at year end (Rs 10 920 interest); Person 2 buys on instalments (12 equal monthly instalments, interest computed on reducing balance; total paid at year end equals Rs 10 920).
Asked: show that the annual interest rate under Person 2's instalment method (computed on the reducing balance, 12 months) is higher than the rate on the loan method. Single proof-style task (rate calculation of both and comparison) - approx 4 + 6 (est.). No diagram. Boxed table of the two methods is the given format.

### A2 - Quadratic function graph y = f(x) (Grade 11)
Context: incomplete table of values for -2<=x<=4 (x: -2..4; y: -5, 0, 3, 4, (blank at x=2), 0, -5), symmetric about x = 1.
- (i) Use symmetry to find the missing y value at x=2 - 1
- (ii) Plot the graph on provided graph paper (choose scale on both axes) - 3
- (iii)(a) Intercepts: write the x-coordinates of the points where graph cuts the x-axis - 1; (b) Using those, write the function in the form y = -(x+p)(x+q) - 1
- (iv) Line y = 1 cuts graph at A and B: (a) range of x for which y > 1 - 2; (b) length of AB to nearest first decimal - 2
Graph paper: not printed on paper (candidate uses own graph sheet); table given.

### A3 - Trigonometry - angles of elevation/depression in a practical context (Grade 11)
Context: a road-race / sloping-ground description: slope surface travelled ascending an inclined plane then another incline; figure (triangle-like diagram: A and E at base, apex D, a point B on AE, C on BD dashed; AC = BE = 50 m, angle CAB = 26 deg 10', BCD perpendicular to AE, DC = BC) provided and to be copied.
- Using trigonometric ratios (tables), show that the difference between the inclination of DE (angle DEB) and the inclination of AC (angle CAB) is greater than 15 degrees. One long proof-style task (marks ~ 10, split est. 4-6).
Diagram: yes (to copy to the answer sheet). Uses trig tables.

### A4 - Quadratic equations & surds (Grade 11)
Context: birthday cake top surface is a right-angled isosceles triangle, equal sides 2x cm; inside, a rectangle for the greeting words with sides (x+2) cm and (x-4) cm, the rest decorated with the cream in area 132 cm^2.
- (i) Show that x satisfies x^2 + 2x - 124 = 0; solve to nearest cm (sqrt5 = 2.24 given) - 5
- (ii) Show the cake's longest edge, 2*sqrt2*x, exceeds 28 cm (sqrt2 = 1.41 given) - 5
Diagram: yes, given (triangle + rectangle).

### A5 - Simultaneous linear equations (Grade 10)
Context: purchase of small and large pigeon-hole style boxes (small boxes holding 10 each and large ones 5 each, price Rs 150 and Rs 120 respectively), total number of boxes 200, total cost Rs 3720 (wording: small/large packs of pigeons).
- (i) Form a pair of simultaneous equations (small = x, large = y) and solve to find how many of each - 5
- (ii) Each small box contains the same number of items; each large box contains 2 more than each small ... given total items 1160, find number of items in a small box - 5
No diagram.

### A6 - Statistics: grouped frequency (mean, expectation) (Grade 10/11)
Context: Number of Kuli (cases) per week for 7 class intervals (5-9, 10-14, ..., 35-39; frequencies 1, 3, 4, 6, 5, 7, 4) provided in a table (classes with 5-wide inclusive limits); three-wheeler driver.
- (i) Mean daily/weekly count (mean number of passenger trips per week) - 3
- (ii) Expected total trips in 52 weeks - 2
- (iii) Given mean trip distance 5 km and income per km, expected income for four weeks - 3
- (iv) Minimum possible total trips in weeks where the number was less than 20 - 2
No diagram; table given.

## 6. Paper II Part B (choose 5 of Q7-12; each 10 marks; sub-part marks not printed)

### B7 - Geometrical constructions (Grade 10-11), 10 marks (est. (i) 2, (ii) 2, (iii) 3, (iv) 2, (v) 1)
Instruction: use only a straight edge with a cm/mm scale and a compass; construction lines must be clearly visible.
- (i) Construct line segment AB = 6 cm; at B construct 60 deg angle with AB and bisect it.
- (ii) Construct isosceles triangle ABC with AB = AC and angle ABC = 30 deg (given).
- (iii) Construct the perpendicular bisector of AC; with AC as diameter construct a semicircle on the side opposite/outside triangle ABC.
- (iv) Mark D where the semicircle meets BC (or a line); construct the line through A parallel to BC to meet the semicircle, name point D, join CD.
- (v) Find angle ACD (reasoning/measure; answer numeric).
No diagram given (constructed by candidate).

### B8 - Geometry - triangles & parallelograms (proof/riders) (Grade 11), 10 marks
Diagram given: parallelogram ABCD, E midpoint of BC, F on DC with DF = (1/3) DC; AB produced and FE produced meet at G; AD produced and EF produced meet at H. (Candidate copies the figure onto the answer sheet.)
- (i) Show triangles BGE and CFE are congruent (reasons: vertically opposite angles, alternate angles, BE = EC) - 2-3
- (ii) Join BF and GC and show BGCF is a parallelogram - 2
- (iii) Show triangles DFH and BGE are equiangular (similar) - 2-3
- (iv) Show DH = (1/4) AD (ratio computation using similarity) - 2-3
Format: all "show that" proofs.

### B9 - Volume of solids, indices/logarithms (Grade 11), 10 marks
Context: 9 identical solid metal pyramids (square base side a, height 2a) are melted without loss to make a solid right cylinder of radius r, height 3r. Pyramid and cylinder volume formulas are given on the cover.
- (i) Show a^3 = (pi/2) r^3 - 4
- (ii) With r = 1.725 and pi = 3.14, find a^3 by logarithms, hence a to the nearest cm - 3
- (iii) A pyramid-shaped piece is cut out of a solid cuboid with square base side a and height 2a; find the remaining metal volume using the value of a - 3
No diagram.

### B10 - Sequences / arithmetic progression application (Grade 10-11), 10 marks
Context: two girls plan to read a novel during the holidays; first one reads 20 pages on day 1, and each next day reads 3 more pages than the previous day (AP, a=20, d=3); second also reads with another rule.
- (i) Write the number of pages read on day 1, 2, 3 (as a list) - 1
- (ii) Pages read on day 16 (nth term) - 2
- (iii) She finishes the book on day 16: the total pages of the book (sum of AP; approximately "equals the sum of first 16 terms") - 2
- (iv) Second girl reads 4 more pages each day than the day before; finishes the book on day 17: how many pages on her first day - 3
- (v) On which day do both read the same number of pages - 2
No diagram; computing questions. (Second part uses a different common difference and same book length.)

### B11 - Circle geometry - tangent, chords, cyclic quadrilateral (Grade 11 proof), 10 marks
Diagram given: circle centre O; AB diameter; chord CD meets AB at X perpendicularly (AB perpendicular to CD); tangent PAR at A; CP perpendicular to PAR. Candidate copies it and joins OC.
- (i) Show PAXC is a cyclic quadrilateral and that AC bisects angle PCO - 5
- (ii) Name two angles equal to angle DAR with reasons (tangent-chord / alternate segment angle) - 5
Style: "show that" proofs with named theorems.

### B12 - Sets: Venn diagram with three sets, counting (Grade 10-11), 10 marks
Diagram given: universal set rectangle with three overlapping circles labelled by arrows with customers who bought (three product types: Milk, Bread-like items, and Rice / one named category each - actually: "milk powder, wheat, rice"-type item categories) shaded centre region; 'a diagram incompletely filled'.
Context: information about 100 customers to a shop; 14 bought none of the three; 60 bought the third item. Candidate copies the diagram and fills in with data.
- (i) Number of customers who bought exactly one of the three item types - 3
- (ii) Number of customers who bought all three if it equals the number who bought exactly two - 3
- (iii) If the number of customers who bought only (item 2 and 3) is twice the number who bought only (1 and 3) - 2
- (iv) If the number who bought item 1 is 52 - find the number who bought item 2 only - 2
Venn diagram partly shaded/labelled; (items: Milk, Bread-like, Rice - labels partly unclear).
